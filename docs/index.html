carry is the entry yield less the cost of funding and roll the price gain from ageing down the curve, both bp per year; per dur is the two together per year of duration. Read that column, not the other two: across this book carry plus roll correlates +0.995 with duration, so ranking on it just ranks by maturity. Per unit of duration the ordering reverses, and today the whole book sits within a few bp of the same number — the curve is charging about the same for every year of risk on it. Carry is quoted at a realistic entry rather than the screen: over the last 20 days trades printed 16bp cheaper than the quote mid. That shift is the same for every bond, so it moves the level of carry and not the ordering.

Rs bn and days are turnover and days traded over the 60 days to 2026-09-21, the last day whose trade file has published — the quote columns run to 2026-09-21, because the two feeds publish on different clocks · auction is days since this bond was last sold, highlighted inside the 14-day window in which freshly auctioned paper has sat about 5bp cheap to its own norm · cover is bids over the amount offered at that auction, so how much demand the last supply met.

gap is distance from the pair's own recent norm · z is that in its own standard deviations · cost is half the bid-offer on each leg · expected is what a signal this size has historically recovered within ten days. Most candidates do not clear their own costs as a standalone round trip — the honest use is choosing between trades you were doing anyway.

A bond's raw distance from the curve is not a signal: some bonds sit permanently cheap. Each is scored against its own trailing 60-day window, which excludes the day being scored. Measured over this sample the residuals mean-revert with a half-life near 6 days; run python -m signals.validate for the current numbers. One 9-month sample in one regime — evidence the mechanism works, not a forecast of what it pays.
